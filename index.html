“Dump my brain so it remembers for me”: An AI-Augmented External Autobiographical Memory for Aphantasia and SDAM

Key message

I supply intent. The agent owns the rest of the memory loop: it gathers, connects, indexes, and surfaces, because I will not pull.

My profile

Aphantasia: no voluntary mental imagery. SDAM (severely deficient autobiographical memory): I cannot relive my own past. Both are self-identified, not diagnosed.

Why memory aids fail me

  • Nothing tells me what to record. Faced with a blank diary, I stall.
  • I never return to read it. The record, and the memory with it, withers.
  • Visual aids form no internal image, and I rarely take photos.

The fix is not more storage but a system that elicits what to capture and returns it when it matters.

How the system works (diagram)

The poster shows a flow diagram with three boxes from left to right: Me (supply intent); AI agent, the largest box (sole reader, writer, organizer; gather, connect, index, surface); and Governed store (rules plus index). A Sources box at the top (messages, work, AI chats, calendar, health) feeds the store through an arrow labeled auto-ingest. Me sends intent to the agent and discusses with it; the agent pushes and surfaces context back to me. The agent records to and indexes the store, and reads the store first before acting. A dashed path from Me directly to the store, labeled “file or browse the store myself”, is marked as removed.

A loop I do not operate. The agent is the store’s only reader, writer, and organizer. The general-purpose agent (Claude) runs interactively and on a schedule, guided by per-folder rule files.

One month of use (2026-05-20 to 2026-06-20)

  • 29 of 32 days with a captured daily entry
  • 46 meeting notes (16 auto-pulled)
  • 174 logged AI working sessions
  • 27 items filed unconfirmed, 5 held back as too ambiguous to place

Descriptive counts from one participant, not efficacy measures. One auto-pulled meeting was misfiled, caught only at weekly review.

Four design principles from daily use

P1, the linchpin: Proactive recall and cross-source connection

Before a meeting, the agent assembled, unasked, the last outcome, open action items, and the messages in between: context from four or five places.

P2: Language and number as the channel

Long, disfluent, mixed-language speech goes in; prose and numbers come out. I read progress off data, such as knee flexion over weeks.

P3: Closure by articulation

I recap aloud; the agent confirms or corrects. One recap caught three misreadings in action items that had looked complete.

P4: Resurfacing dispersed threads

A question raised, set aside, and reopened over weeks was rebuilt into what was settled, rejected, and still open.

Connection is where the deficit bites

For a typical worker, fragmented context is a tax paid in toggling; the reconnecting still happens in the head. Mine does not, so the prosthesis must supply it. One risk does not invert: a confidently wrong output I cannot check against my own memory.

Limits

  • N = 1, designer as participant.
  • Needs a high digital footprint; a four-day trip cut capture to a fifth.
  • Consent wins over coverage: nothing is recorded at home.
